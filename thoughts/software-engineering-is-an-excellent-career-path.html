software engineering is actually a pretty excellent career path methinks

October 25, 2026

i'm convinced more than ever that being a good engineer is useful and necessary.

as ai becomes a force multiplier on what an engineer can do, the effect a single engineer can have is measurably larger than it was in the past.

i'm also convinced that making / developing good engineers is increasingly more difficult. moments of difficulty that would force engineers to get better are now functionally non-existent (or can be). if we want claude to fill in the gaps or help us get over the hill... we can do so and get away with it.

but in doing that we are losing the opportunity to get better. unfortunately, i think this will increasingly be the common case (this study is related to schools, but the schools aren't pushing AI on students, companies actively are, so this effect will be even more magnified at companies in the coming years).

which means the median engineer in the industry will become increasingly worse.

which is a bad thing for the software industry.

but i guess does provide an opportunity for those who do care enough to become excellent engineers.

concurrently, as AI can increasingly do what a junior engineer used to do, there will be increasingly less roles for junior developers.

as doom and gloom around this fact increases, there will be increasingly less students who decide to pursue careers in software engineering (this has already started happening at umich).

so my hypothesis is this: we'll increasingly have less and less engineers, and we'll increasingly have worse median engineers.

that means, in somewhere like 3-7 years (assuming AI hasn't solved engineering, which isn't impossible, but i think it's unlikely) i think there'll be some of the biggest talent droughts the industry has ever seen.
