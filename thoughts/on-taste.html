on taste

October 29, 2026

i think the very stark reality that ai is just better than most people at most cognitive things has made people kinda look for straws to grasp.

like what's my productive value in society if there's no longer a need for me to be able to do this cognitively difficult thing? many people have tied their identity to being particularly good at something (myself included to a certain extent), and it's pretty sad that like you're just not as competent as a box of wires at it.

and i think a lot of the discourse in the culture has shifted to having "good taste"

honestly though, i think it's kinda lame. the average person by definition will have average views. if ai is trained on the views of all humans (generalization but not really), then ai will also be able to pretty reliably come up with the "taste" that an average person has. rl will improve its taste to the extent it can be improved (what's a verifiable reward for taste?).

i think the fact that taste is subjective is like some indirection to be able to shield you from the fact that ai is just better than you.

idk like i encourage you to think hard about it: when's the last time you had an original thought that wasn't contrived or copied or useless. frankly (and unfortunately), in the common case most people are bots. i'm pretty sure i'm a bot too, but idk at least i'm self-aware. if that counts for anything. but on a real note i think the bot epidemic mostly related to constant media consumption.

anyway the point that i'm making is like you can try to develop taste*, but like realistically you're not rick rubin or rick owens or even rick ross. you're just a claude wrapper, kinda reminds me of this.

i do think it's like a good thing to try to develop yk. travel the world, read hemingway and dostoevsky and morrison, listen to classic music and jazz. idk become cultured ig.

but if that's the new thing that signals value in the zeitgeist, then i think it'll end up being more of Goodhart's Law than people actually becoming interesting. the very act of trying to become interesting could be performative.

probably not the worst thing in the world though.. definitely beats being a bot.

* i guess i should mention that i define taste as having very informed, opinionated feelings about what you like and what you don't. ↩
